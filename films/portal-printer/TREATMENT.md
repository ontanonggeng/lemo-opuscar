# BLACKSTAR — We Just Needed One Shipping Label

## Production treatment

**Format:** original vertical animated sci-fi sitcom Short  
**Style:** Sci-Fi Sitcom Toon, implemented against `styles/scifi-toon/STYLE.md`  
**Target:** 42–45 seconds, 1080×1920, 24 fps, US English  
**Voice:** offline Kokoro, two contrasting US-English voices  
**Brand:** BLACKSTAR v5 corner watermark  
**Publishing:** never automatic

## Three candidate structures

### A. Portal Printer — selected
Two warehouse workers need one ordinary shipping label. Their battered label printer opens a dimensional portal every time it prints, cycling through increasingly wrong destinations. When the correct Ohio label finally appears, the printer teleports the package away — then immediately prints a return label and brings it back.

**Why it works:** one mundane goal, one escalating device, strong prop comedy, multiple palette changes, clear reaction shots, a rule-of-three acceleration, and a callback ending. The device is household-office junk used for absurdly powerful sci-fi work.

### B. Microwave Time Card
Two night-shift workers heat one leftover burrito in a microwave that skips the food through different times of day. Each return is increasingly overcooked until the microwave sends the worker's lunch break itself into the future.

**Why not selected:** strong one-world-many-clocks move, but visually closer to the previous apartment/device Short and gives less opportunity for world-palette contrast.

### C. Interdimensional Lost-and-Found
A clerk tries to return a single lost glove while a filing cabinet opens portals to increasingly implausible owners. The final owner is an alternate version of the clerk.

**Why not selected:** good character reversal, but more dialogue-heavy and slower to understand in the first two seconds.

## Selected logline
At a night-shift warehouse, deadpan supervisor Marn and nervous temp Kip only need one shipping label, but their portal-equipped printer keeps opening the wrong worlds — until it finally ships the box correctly and instantly issues a return.

## Arc
**Setup:** A grotesque little creature drops out of a portal above a label printer. Kip correctly observes that this is not Ohio.  
**Escalation:** Marn insists the printer is merely misconfigured. Two more destination attempts reveal wildly different worlds and creatures.  
**Turn:** Kip selects LOCAL DELIVERY. The machine suddenly becomes calm and prints a perfectly normal Ohio label.  
**Ending:** The package is swallowed by the printer portal and marked delivered. A beat later the printer emits RETURN TO SENDER and spits the same package back, now visibly mauled. Marn silently tears off the return label and says, “Truck it is.”

## Character design

### Marn
Short, broad adult woman; warm brown skin; blunt dark bob with one shaved side; heavy-lidded white eyes; tiny pupils. Wears a **mustard hazard skirt over slate cargo trousers**, a dark teal utility vest, and huge square-toed safety boots. Her silhouette is triangular and grounded. She barely moves unless she has decided something.

**Performance:** low, flat, slow; precise mouth shapes; one eyebrow does most of the acting. She reacts to absurdity with stillness.

### Kip
Very tall, narrow adult man; medium skin; long nose; messy swept-up hair; large touching eyes; dark circles. Wears a **puffy violet inflatable safety vest over a cream warehouse uniform**, with one oversized scanner holster and skinny boots. His silhouette is vertical and twitchy.

**Performance:** faster, higher, nervous; pupils dart; shoulders rise before gestures; hands anticipate every button press.

### Parcel creature
Non-verbal, disgusting-cute four-eyed sluglet with a taped cardboard scrap stuck to its back. It appears only through the printer portal and communicates with wet chirps. It must never resemble an existing copyrighted character.

## Device design — PORTA-LABEL 88
A battered thermal shipping-label printer mounted on a dented rolling cart, with a crooked keyboard, hand-written calibration notes, dangling cable loops, and a lumpy dimensional aperture where the paper tray should be.

The **portal is the only glowing object in the film**. It uses a pale electric cyan core, two flat translucent rings, thick dark outline, spiral arms, orbiting sparks and two dripping lobes. No gradients.

## Palette logic

### Warehouse / home world
- sage grey-green walls
- rust-orange safety markings
- cream paper/cardboard
- charcoal-violet ink
- no cyan except the portal

### Wrong World 1 — Frost Freight
- deep cobalt
- coral
- dirty white
- charcoal-violet ink

### Wrong World 2 — Jelly Customs
- magenta
- acid yellow-green
- deep plum
- charcoal-violet ink

### Wrong World 3 — Suspiciously Normal Office
- beige
- coffee brown
- dusty grey
- charcoal-violet ink

The line colour and eye white never change. The portal cyan never appears anywhere else.

## Beat sheet

- **0–2s:** portal already active; creature falls out; hook line.
- **2–6s:** deadpan rationalization.
- **6–13s:** wrong world one + reaction.
- **13–21s:** wrong world two + “normal printer” joke.
- **21–27s:** rapid rule-of-three destination failures.
- **27–32.5s:** suspicious calm; correct label.
- **32.5–38.5s:** successful teleport delivery.
- **38.5–44s:** silence, return label, ruined parcel, final line.

## Camera grammar
Use at least four deliberate moves: slow push, portal rush zoom, whip pan, repeated same-framing world swap, and final slow push. No dissolves. Every absurd world reveal is followed by a reaction shot. Characters stay out of the subtitle band.

## Motion grammar
Characters animate on twos at 12 fps: poses, blinks, pupils, mouths and boil. Portal swirl, flying paper, box motion and camera moves animate on ones at 24 fps. Every meaningful hand gesture uses anticipation, action and follow-through. Held props sit between palms; scanner and labels maintain contact continuity.

## Subtitle design
Bottom-centre heavy rounded sans, white with thick dark outline; speaker pill in character colour on the left. Maximum two lines. Subtitles clip before silent beats so the cold pauses remain visually clean.

## Score and sound
Original synthesized sci-fi score only:
- warehouse: low analog bass + restrained square arpeggio
- Frost Freight: brittle high theremin notes
- Jelly Customs: rubbery chromatic synth
- montage: accelerating drum-machine rejection pattern
- correct-label beat: hard cut to room tone
- final return: one mechanical print sound, then a short dry sting after Marn’s line

Foley: thermal printer chatter, portal sub-boom/swirl/fwoomp, wet slug chirp, frozen crackle, jelly stamp squish, scanner beep, cardboard scrape, suction rush, box impact.

## Style compliance gates
Before the full render, prove the look with:
1. Marn model sheet: silhouette, 3/4 face, three expressions, reach pose.
2. Kip model sheet: silhouette, 3/4 face, three expressions, panic pose.
3. Printer/device sheet with portal closed/open.
4. Two actual-code style frames: S01 hook and S09 signature shot.

Reject the look if it resembles a vector explainer, uses gradients, has thin/scaled outlines, lacks line boil, uses glow outside the portal, produces static unacted characters, or copies an existing show’s silhouettes/colour pairings.

Treatment first. After this file exists, inspect the upstream `scifi-toon` demo only for rendering technique and motion grammar. The film must not reuse its story, cast, shots, devices, world sequence, dialogue or ending.
